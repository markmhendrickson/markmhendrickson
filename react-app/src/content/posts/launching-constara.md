Today I’m launching [Constara](https://constara.ai). I set up and run agents for service businesses, online retailers, and other teams that want to handle more work while keeping control of what goes out and who makes the decisions. The agents prepare the recurring steps. Your team decides what they can do, where they have to wait, and who steps in when a case needs judgment.

The work could be answering an inquiry, bringing a customer back, handling a return, responding to a review, checking an overdue invoice, or putting the week’s numbers together. A clinic, fitness club, online retailer, or agency may need different workflows. Each has to decide what’s worth handing over, what the agent should prepare, and when a person needs to take over.

## What changes when agents do the repetitive steps

A customer can write through a web form in the morning and send a follow-up to the business number in the afternoon. If those messages live in separate inboxes, someone has to connect them before they can answer. At the end of the week, another person may have to piece together which inquiries turned into bookings and which still need attention.

Constara brings information from the sources you agree on into a shared record. An agent can use it to prepare a reply or reminder and put it in front of the person who can decide what goes out. If the case needs a judgment call, that person can see what the customer asked, what the team already told them, and what’s still unresolved. The same record gives the owner a weekly view of what moved and which figures are still missing.

## Where to start

Start with a recurring job your team already knows well. An agency might choose [new-business follow-up](https://constara.ai/en/new-business-follow-up/) after a consultation, when an inquiry can get lost between the first conversation and a decision. A fitness club might start with existing members by noticing [when attendance drops](https://constara.ai/en/use-case-attendance-drop-off/) or preparing renewals. A clinic might choose [no-show recovery](https://constara.ai/en/use-case-no-show-recovery/) so reception can offer another appointment, or prepare routine check-ins in an agreed care plan. The team sets the timing and wording, and clinical questions go to a clinician.

The delay might be somewhere else entirely. When a shop [handles a return or exchange](https://constara.ai/en/use-case-returns-and-exchanges/), it needs the order and the policy before it can reply. A team following up on [unpaid invoices](https://constara.ai/en/use-case-unpaid-invoice-follow-up/) needs the payment and dispute history before sending a reminder. For [review requests](https://constara.ai/en/use-case-review-requests/), the team can agree when to ask and who should handle feedback that needs a personal response.

The same approach works inside the business. A [weekly owner report](https://constara.ai/en/use-case-weekly-owner-report/) can show which figures have a source and which are missing. A team could also use meeting notes to keep track of pending work and make it clear who owns each commitment.

## A worked example from a dental clinic

At a fictional dental clinic, reception chooses to start with a job it already handles every day. A new patient’s inquiry shows how the agent and the team share the work from first contact through booking.

The agent can prepare replies and reminders from the information the clinic has approved. Reception decides what gets sent. If a patient includes health details in a message, it goes directly to clinic staff for a reply.

### Step 1. The inquiry arrives

A new patient asks the clinic about an evening appointment through its web form. The inquiry enters the shared record with its source.

If the patient calls the business number next, reception can pick up from the original question. The same record also gives a colleague enough context to take over the case.

![A speech card, notes, and an envelope sit on a brass rail above an empty tray](/images/posts/constara-paper/rail-step-1.webp "paper-step||The clinic can see what the patient asked and which channel it came through.")

### Step 2. The agent prepares a reply

For this booking inquiry, the agent prepares a reply from the clinic’s approved information about prices, availability, and common questions. Reception can read the customer’s question and the draft together.

If the draft leaves a question open, reception can check what the agent used. That makes it easier to see whether the draft needs a correction or the clinic needs to supply more information.

![An open envelope, a drafted letter, and a tray of index cards](/images/posts/constara-paper/rail-step-2.webp "paper-step||The agent drafts from the clinic’s approved answers and availability.")

### Step 3. A person decides what goes out

Before the reply goes out, a named person checks it. If an evening slot has filled since the draft was prepared, reception can offer a different time. If the patient has a clinical question, reception can hold the booking reply and pass the question to the clinician. The same checkpoint gives staff a place to make a simple edit or take over a more consequential decision.

This is also where the clinic’s normal judgment enters the workflow. The person who approves can see whether the draft covers a booking question within the agreed scope or requires someone with clinical responsibility to respond.

![Paper cards on a rail divided by a red line, with a stamp on the far side](/images/posts/constara-paper/rail-step-3.webp "paper-step||Reception can edit, send, or stop the prepared reply.")

### Step 4. Reception follows up

If the patient hasn’t chosen a time, the job stays open. The agent can prepare a reminder on the clinic’s schedule.

If the patient declines, the team can close the case and record the outcome. If an insurer or clinical question comes up, reception has the context to pass it to the right person.

![Calendar cards run along a brass rail, with one card diverted toward a tray](/images/posts/constara-paper/rail-step-4.webp "paper-step||Follow-up stays on schedule, and unusual replies go back to a named person.")

### Step 5. The visit is booked and recorded

When the visit is booked, the record keeps the outcome alongside the inquiry, the reply, and the approval. The next person can pick up the conversation from that history.

The same booking can count toward the weekly figures. If the count changes, the team can find the booking behind it without reconstructing the case from the inbox.

![A dark record book hangs from a rail with a paper slip tucked inside](/images/posts/constara-paper/rail-step-5.webp "paper-step||The record keeps the inquiry, the reply, the approval, and the booking together.")

## How the system keeps work accountable

Constara runs on two systems I build and use for my own work. [Ateles](https://markmhendrickson.com/posts/the-swarm-that-runs-my-work) defines who does each step, what an agent can do, and when it must wait for a person. [Neotoma](https://neotoma.io/) keeps each result with its source and correction history. Put those together and a new person can see why a task is waiting, and an agent preparing a follow-up can use the last decision the team made.

Constara runs the agreed workflow alongside the AI tools your team already uses. Before work starts, the proposal names which sources are in scope, what actions are allowed, and where a person has to step in. If a later engagement requires access to personal data, both parties sign a [data processing agreement](https://constara.ai/en/trust-and-data/) before access begins. Those details belong in the scope of each job, where your team can check them.

> **Who approves outgoing messages**
>
> **Nothing goes out without a checkpoint. In the pilot, a named person on your team approves every message before it is sent. Later, routine messages can go out on templates you have approved in writing, but anything that touches health or a clinical matter always comes back to a person.**

## Why I built it

I started building Neotoma in November 2025 for my own work. I wanted an agent helping me with a personal or professional task to pick up from the record I had already made and give me a way to check or correct it. I [released Neotoma for developers](https://markmhendrickson.com/posts/neotoma-developer-release/) in February 2026 so other people who run their own tools could try it.

When I talked with business owners, the need was concrete. A follow-up should reflect what the customer was already told. The rest of the team should be able to see who replied and what still needs attention. The owners wanted those jobs taken care of without having to install, connect, and maintain the tools themselves.

I began building Ateles in May 2026 and developed it through June as I moved from directing one agent at a time to giving agents distinct roles in my own work. Once I could give each agent a job and set where it had to wait for me, I could see what was missing for those owners. Someone had to run the workflow with the people already doing the work. The team needed a clear way to follow it and step in. That’s the service I’m building with Constara.

> **What makes the work dependable**
>
> **Models can read an inquiry and draft a reply. Ateles coordinates work across people and agents, applies the permissions your team sets, and pauses when a person needs to approve an action. Neotoma keeps a shared record of what’s current, where it came from, and what changed. Together, they let your team pick up work across shifts and tools, see what’s already been decided, and check the evidence before acting.**

## Customer service and back-office work

For a retailer handling an order question, the person replying needs to see what the customer asked, what they ordered, and what the shipping record says. A shared record gives staff that history in one place. An agent can bring the relevant details together so the person handling the case can decide how to respond.

The same setup can help an agency with work inside the team. When a colleague takes over a lead, they can see the last contact and what still needs doing. When a draft goes into review, the reviewer can open the source material and see what changed since the last version. In either case, there’s a clear place to pick up the work.

The retailer’s next decision is about what to tell a customer. The agency’s might be who will follow up or whether a draft is ready for review. Both need the record of the work that led up to that decision.

I want a small team to be able to start with a job it can point to today. That could be a queue of returns, a weekly report someone assembles by hand, or reminders that go out late.
